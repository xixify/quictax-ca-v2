## Info sections (6 cards)

### Key Filing Deadlines
Personal tax returns are due April 30 each year. Self-employed individuals get until June 15, but any taxes owed are still due April 30. Missing deadlines triggers late-filing penalties and daily compound interest.

### How the CRA Calculates What You Owe
The CRA uses a progressive tax system — the more you earn, the higher your marginal rate. Federal rates range from 15% on the first $55,867 up to 33% on income over $246,752 (2024). Provincial rates stack on top.

### CRA My Account
CRA My Account lets you view your tax return status, RRSP contribution room, benefit payments, and notices of assessment online. QuicTax can help you set it up and interpret what you see.

### Audits & Reviews
The CRA selects returns for review based on risk factors like large deductions, self-employment income, or inconsistencies with prior years. QuicTax prepares every return audit-ready with proper documentation.

### Benefits & Credits
The CRA administers the GST/HST credit, Canada Child Benefit, Climate Action Incentive, and more. Filing on time — even with zero income — is required to receive these payments.

### Reassessments & Adjustments
Made a mistake on a past return? The CRA allows adjustments going back 10 years via a T1-ADJ. QuicTax can review prior-year returns and recover missed deductions or credits.

## "Did You Know?" facts (rotating/carousel items)

- → The CRA processes over 30 million individual tax returns every year.
- → You can carry forward unused RRSP contribution room indefinitely — it never expires.
- → The CRA charges compound daily interest on unpaid balances — currently at the prescribed rate plus 4%.
- → Voluntary Disclosure allows Canadians to correct past errors without facing penalties, if filed before the CRA contacts you.
