# Tax Filing Deadlines Canadians Cannot Miss

Missing tax filing deadlines is rarely just a calendar problem. For many Canadians, it can mean late-filing penalties, daily interest on a balance owing, delayed benefits, or a last-minute scramble to find receipts that should have been organized months ago. The good news: most deadlines are predictable. Once you know which one applies to you, filing becomes a simple job with a clear finish line.

This guide covers the main Canadian deadlines for the 2025 tax year, filed in 2026. Your situation matters. An employee with a T4, a freelancer with business income, a corporation, and a business registered for GST/HST do not all follow the same schedule.

## Personal tax filing deadlines for 2026

For most individuals, the deadline to file a 2025 personal income tax return is **April 30, 2026**. This is also the deadline to pay any income tax balance you owe for 2025.

Filing on time matters even if you cannot pay the full amount immediately. The [Canada Revenue Agency](https://quictax.ca/cra) can charge a late-filing penalty when you file after the deadline and owe tax. Interest can also begin accumulating on unpaid amounts. Filing first gives you a clear number, keeps your account current, and lets you deal with payment options based on facts rather than estimates.

If you expect a refund, there is generally no late-filing penalty. Still, waiting can delay money that belongs in your account. It can also hold up income-tested benefits and credits, including payments that rely on your most recent tax return.

### What to prepare before April 30

A clean filing starts with complete documents. Employees will usually need T4 slips, while people with investments, tuition, pension income, medical expenses, charitable donations, childcare costs, or moving expenses may have additional records to provide.

Do not assume every slip is the whole story. Your tax return may also need information about work-from-home expenses, eligible professional dues, rental income, capital gains, or a spouse's income. The right deduction depends on your actual circumstances and supporting documents. Claiming everything without proof is not a strategy. Accurate records are.

## Self-employed tax filing deadlines

If you or your spouse or common-law partner was self-employed in 2025, the return filing deadline is **June 15, 2026**. This can apply to freelancers, consultants, drivers, creators, contractors, sole proprietors, and gig workers.

There is one detail that catches people every year: the payment deadline does **not** move to June 15. Any 2025 balance owing is still due by **April 30, 2026**. File by June 15 if you qualify, but pay by April 30 to avoid interest on the amount owed.

This difference matters because self-employed income often has no tax withheld at source. If you had a strong year, your balance may be larger than expected. Waiting until June to calculate it can create an unnecessary cash-flow problem.

### Build your business-income file early

Self-employed tax filing is more than entering revenue. You need to separate business income from personal transfers and identify reasonable expenses related to earning that income. Common categories include supplies, advertising, software, phone use, professional fees, vehicle expenses, home office costs, and subcontractor payments.

The deduction is not automatically the full amount on every receipt. A mixed-use expense, such as a mobile phone or vehicle, may need to be divided between business and personal use. Home office expenses also depend on how the space is used. Good records make these decisions faster, more accurate, and easier to support if questions arise later.

If you are registered for GST/HST, keep that information separate from your income-tax records. GST/HST collected is not simply extra revenue, and input tax credits should be tracked with the invoices and receipts that support them.

## Corporate tax filing deadlines

Corporations follow a different timeline. A T2 corporate income tax return is generally due **six months after the end of the corporation's fiscal year**. If your corporation has a December 31, 2025 year-end, the filing deadline is June 30, 2026.

The corporate tax payment deadline can arrive sooner. Many corporations must pay their balance within two months of fiscal year-end. Some Canadian-controlled private corporations that meet specific conditions may have three months. The exact rule depends on your corporation's status, taxable income, and other factors.

That gap between payment and filing is a major reason corporate owners should not treat the filing deadline as the only deadline. If you wait until month six to review your books, you may already have missed the payment date.

### A year-end is not just a paperwork date

Corporate compliance works best when bookkeeping is current throughout the year. Before filing, revenue, expenses, shareholder transactions, payroll records, GST/HST activity, and bank reconciliations need to line up. A rushed return can lead to missed deductions, incorrect balances, or extra time spent resolving inconsistencies.

Corporate owners should also consider whether they received dividends, salary, management fees, or shareholder advances. These items can affect both the corporation and the individual owner's personal return. Getting the timing right helps prevent surprises on either side.

## GST/HST filing deadlines depend on your reporting period

There is no single GST/HST deadline for every business. Your due date depends on whether you file monthly, quarterly, or annually.

Monthly filers generally file and pay one month after the end of each reporting period. Quarterly filers generally file and pay one month after the end of each quarter. Annual filers often have a filing deadline three months after fiscal year-end, although some individuals with a December 31 year-end may have a June filing deadline with an April payment deadline.

Because the details can vary, check the reporting period assigned to your GST/HST account rather than relying on a date another business owner uses. A contractor filing quarterly and a corporation filing annually can both be registered for GST/HST while facing very different deadlines.

The practical rule is simple: do not spend GST/HST collected as if it were business profit. Set it aside. When you claim input tax credits, keep the supporting receipts and make sure the tax was actually paid or payable on eligible business purchases.

## Tax installments: the deadlines that arrive before tax season

Some taxpayers are required to make income tax installment payments during the year. The usual installment dates are **March 15, June 15, September 15, and December 15**.

Installments are common for self-employed people, landlords, investors, and anyone who regularly owes significant tax that is not withheld through payroll. The CRA may send installment reminders, but the obligation is based on your tax situation, not just whether a reminder reaches you.

Installments can feel frustrating because they require planning before your return is filed. In practice, they can make cash flow easier. Instead of facing one large balance in April, you spread payments across the year. The trade-off is that you need a reliable system for saving tax money as income arrives.

## What to do when you are close to a deadline

If April 30, June 15, or another due date is approaching, do not wait for every detail to feel perfect. Start by gathering the records you have and identifying what is missing. A missing slip or unclear expense can often be resolved faster when the rest of the return is already organized.

If you owe tax but cannot pay in full, file on time whenever possible. A filed return gives you a confirmed balance and can reduce the damage compared with filing late. You may still owe interest, but avoiding a late-filing penalty can make a meaningful difference.

For a straightforward return, early preparation is usually enough. For self-employed income, GST/HST, rental activity, or corporate records, give yourself more room. The more moving parts you have, the less useful last-minute filing becomes.

## A faster way to stay on track

The easiest deadline is the one you prepare for before it becomes urgent. Create one secure folder for tax slips, invoices, expense receipts, mileage records, GST/HST documents, and prior-year returns. Add to it throughout the year instead of rebuilding your financial history in April.
