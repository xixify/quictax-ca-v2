# How to Register for HST When Your Business Is Ready

Your business can go from a side hustle to an HST registrant faster than expected. One strong month, a new client contract, or steady online sales may put you near the $30,000 threshold. Knowing **how to register for HST** before you cross that line helps you avoid late-registration issues, missed tax credits, and awkward conversations with customers about adding tax after the fact.

HST registration is not difficult, but the timing matters. Register too late and you may owe tax you did not collect. Register voluntarily too early and you take on filing and recordkeeping duties before they are worthwhile. The right move depends on your revenue, expenses, location, and business plans.

## When Do You Need to Register for HST?

Most Canadian businesses must register for a GST/HST account once they stop qualifying as a small supplier. In general, you are no longer a small supplier when your taxable revenue exceeds $30,000 in either a single calendar quarter or over four consecutive calendar quarters.

This is based on revenue, not profit. If you earn $35,000 in sales but spend $25,000 on software, supplies, contractors, and advertising, you may still need to register. The threshold looks at taxable supplies, which generally include most sales of goods and services in Canada.

The calculation can become more complicated if you operate through related or associated businesses. For example, splitting activities between two companies does not automatically let you treat each business as separate for threshold purposes. If that sounds close to your situation, get professional advice before assuming you are below the limit.

Once you exceed $30,000 in a single quarter, registration is generally required right away. If you exceed it across four consecutive quarters, you usually need to register by the end of the month following the quarter in which you stopped being a small supplier. You then need to begin charging GST or HST from your effective registration date.

## Should You Register Voluntarily Before $30,000?

You can choose to register even when your sales are below the mandatory threshold. For freelancers, consultants, contractors, and small businesses with meaningful startup expenses, voluntary registration can make sense because it allows you to claim input tax credits.

Input tax credits let you recover GST/HST paid on eligible business purchases. That can include equipment, office supplies, software subscriptions, professional fees, advertising, inventory, and some home office costs. If you are buying a laptop, tools, inventory, or other taxable business items, those credits can reduce the tax you remit.

There is a trade-off. After voluntary registration, you must charge the appropriate tax on taxable sales, keep proper records, file GST/HST returns, and remit any balance owing on time. Registration is not just a way to get money back on expenses. It creates an ongoing compliance obligation.

Voluntary registration is often useful when your clients are businesses that can claim their own input tax credits. They may care more about your work and invoice quality than the tax added to your bill. It can be less appealing if most of your customers are consumers, because HST may raise their final out-of-pocket price.

## What You Need Before You Register for HST

A little preparation makes the registration process much faster. You will need your legal business name, mailing address, business activity, ownership details, and the date you want your GST/HST account to take effect.

You will also need a business number, often called a BN. If you already have one for payroll or corporate income tax, your GST/HST account will be added under that existing nine-digit number. The GST/HST program account normally ends in RT0001.

Choose your effective registration date carefully. If you crossed the mandatory threshold, the date is driven by the rules. If you are registering voluntarily, you can generally choose a date that fits your operations. From that date forward, you are responsible for charging tax where required and can generally claim eligible input tax credits.

It also helps to know your expected annual taxable revenue. The Canada Revenue Agency uses this information to assign a reporting period, although you may be able to elect a different schedule in some cases. Annual filers have fewer returns to prepare, while quarterly reporting can help a growing business stay closer to its cash flow and avoid a large year-end tax balance.

## How to Register for HST With the CRA

The fastest way to register is usually through the CRA's [online business registration service](https://quictax.ca/cra). You can also register by phone, by mail, or through an authorized representative. The online option is practical for many sole proprietors and new businesses because it lets you provide the core registration details in one place.

When completing the application, select the GST/HST program account and provide your registration effective date, estimated revenues, business activity, and reporting-period information. Review every answer before submitting. A wrong effective date can create unnecessary returns or affect when you should have started charging tax.

After registration, you will receive confirmation of your GST/HST account number. Add that number to your invoices and update your bookkeeping system immediately. Your invoices should clearly show the amount of GST/HST charged, the applicable rate, and your registration number when required.

Keep a copy of the registration confirmation with your business records. You will need the account details when filing returns, making payments, speaking with the CRA, or giving information to your bookkeeper or tax preparer.

## Charge the Right Tax After Registration

Registering for HST does not mean every invoice gets the same rate. The tax you charge can depend on what you sell, where your customer is located, and the place-of-supply rules.

Ontario uses a 13% HST rate. New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Prince Edward Island generally use 15% HST. Nova Scotia currently uses a 14% HST rate. Businesses selling into Alberta, British Columbia, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Quebec, or the territories may need to charge GST only or deal with separate provincial sales tax requirements, depending on the transaction.

Service businesses often assume the rate is based only on their own location. That is not always true. An Ontario consultant working remotely for a client in another province may need to apply a different rate based on the applicable place-of-supply rules. Digital products, cross-border work, real estate, and certain specialized services can add further complexity.

Some supplies are zero-rated or exempt. Zero-rated sales are taxable at 0%, which can still allow input tax credits. Exempt sales are treated differently and may restrict the credits you can claim. Health care, education, financial services, and certain residential rental arrangements are common areas where the rules need a closer look.

## Keep Records That Make Filing Easier

Good HST compliance starts with clean records, not with a stressful filing deadline. Save sales invoices, receipts, supplier bills, import documents, bank statements, and records supporting every input tax credit you claim. Your books should separate tax collected from tax paid on business expenses.

Do not treat all expenses as automatically claimable. Personal purchases, entertainment costs, and expenses related to exempt activities may be restricted or ineligible. For mixed business and personal costs, such as a cellphone or home internet, claim only the business portion supported by reasonable records.

Set aside the tax you collect instead of treating it as operating cash. HST collected from customers is generally money you hold for the government until your return is filed. A separate savings account can make remittances much less painful, especially during busy sales periods.

## File Your GST/HST Returns on Time

Your return reports the GST/HST you collected and the input tax credits you are eligible to claim. If you collected more tax than you paid on eligible expenses, you remit the difference. If your eligible credits are higher, you may receive a refund.

Missing a filing deadline can lead to penalties and interest, even if you have no balance owing. File every required return, including nil returns where no tax was collected or claimed. If you discover an error after filing, address it promptly rather than hoping it disappears in a later return.
