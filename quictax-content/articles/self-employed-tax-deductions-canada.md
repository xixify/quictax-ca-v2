# Self Employed Tax Deductions Canada: What Counts

That laptop, phone bill, client lunch, and mileage log may reduce your taxable business income - but only when the expense is genuinely connected to earning that income. For freelancers and small-business owners, understanding self employed tax deductions Canada allows can mean keeping more of what you earn without creating problems at filing time.

The goal is not to claim every purchase that could possibly relate to work. It is to claim reasonable business costs, keep clear records, and separate personal spending from business spending. Get those three things right and tax season becomes much less stressful.

## Self employed tax deductions Canada allows: the basic rule

The [Canada Revenue Agency](https://quictax.ca/cra) generally allows you to deduct expenses you paid to earn business or professional income. The expense must be reasonable in the circumstances. A graphic designer can deduct design software used for client work. A rideshare driver can claim the business portion of eligible vehicle costs. A consultant can claim professional liability insurance.

Personal expenses do not become deductible just because you are self-employed. A regular suit, your family grocery bill, or the drive from home to a regular workplace is usually personal. Where an expense has both business and personal use, claim only the business share.

This is where many returns go wrong. The deduction is not necessarily all or nothing. Your internet, cell phone, vehicle, and home workspace may be legitimate claims, but they usually require a fair allocation.

## Common deductions worth tracking

### Supplies, software, and professional services

Small items consumed while running your business are usually current expenses. This can include office supplies, packaging, printing, cleaning materials, and small tools. Digital costs count too when they support your work, such as bookkeeping software, cloud storage, a website platform, video meeting subscriptions, or industry-specific applications.

You may also deduct reasonable fees paid for legal advice, bookkeeping, tax preparation, advertising, payment processing, and professional memberships. If you pay an accountant to prepare your business return or help with HST compliance, retain the invoice.

Advertising can include online ads, business cards, promotional materials, and sponsorships connected to your business. Keep a note of what each larger campaign was for. A short description can make a receipt far easier to explain months later.

### Phone and internet costs

A phone plan is not automatically 100% deductible because clients sometimes call you. If you use your phone 60% for business and 40% personally, claim 60% of the eligible cost. The same idea applies to home internet.

A practical approach is to use a dedicated business number, separate email, or a business-only mobile plan when your work volume justifies it. It makes your records cleaner and the business-use percentage easier to support. You do not need to create extra complexity just for tax purposes, but a clear trail saves time.

### Home office expenses

Working from the kitchen table occasionally does not always create a home office deduction. Generally, your workspace must be your principal place of business, or it must be used only to earn business income and used regularly and continuously to meet clients, customers, or patients.

If you qualify, you can claim a reasonable portion of costs such as rent, utilities, home internet, minor repairs, and certain other eligible expenses. Homeowners may have access to different categories, including mortgage interest in some situations, but mortgage principal is not deductible.

Your claim is usually based on the workspace's share of your home. Square footage is a common method. If the room is also used personally, adjust again for time. For example, a spare room used exclusively as an office can support a stronger claim than a dining room used for work during the day and family meals at night.

Home office claims have limits. They generally cannot create or increase a business loss, although unused eligible amounts may be carried forward in some cases. This is one area where accurate calculations matter more than a rough estimate.

### Vehicle expenses and mileage

If you use your vehicle to visit clients, pick up supplies, travel to temporary job sites, or deliver products, you may claim the business portion of eligible costs. Depending on your situation, that can include fuel, insurance, maintenance, repairs, licensing, lease payments, or capital cost allowance for an owned vehicle.

The critical record is your mileage log. Track total kilometers driven during the year and business kilometers driven. Your claim is based on the business-use percentage, not just on how expensive the car is.

Commuting from home to a regular place of work is normally personal travel. Driving from one client location to another, or from your office to buy business supplies, is different. Parking related to business travel may be deductible, while parking for your ordinary commute usually is not.

### Meals, travel, and client meetings

Business travel can be deductible when it is necessary for earning income. Think transportation, accommodation, and other reasonable travel costs for a client engagement, conference, or out-of-town work assignment. Keep the receipt and record the business purpose, destination, dates, and who attended.

Meals and entertainment are commonly limited to 50% of the eligible amount. A restaurant bill without context is weak documentation. Add a brief note: “Project planning meeting with Client A” or “Lunch during out-of-town installation.” The note takes seconds and helps establish why the expense belongs in your books.

Do not treat a vacation as a business trip because you answered emails beside the pool. A trip with both personal and business purposes requires a careful split.

## Bigger purchases are treated differently

A computer, camera, furniture, machinery, or vehicle may provide value for more than one year. These are often capital assets, not immediate expenses. Rather than deducting the full purchase price at once, you may claim capital cost allowance over time under the applicable tax rules.

The distinction matters. Expensing a major equipment purchase in full when it should be capitalized can overstate your deductions. On the other hand, missing capital cost allowance means you may leave a legitimate tax benefit unclaimed. The right treatment depends on the asset, its cost, and how it is used.

## HST is not simply another expense deduction

If you are registered for GST/HST, the tax you pay on eligible business purchases may often be recoverable through input tax credits. That is separate from claiming the underlying expense on your income tax return.

For example, if you buy business software and pay HST, you generally should not claim the HST twice - once as part of the expense and again as an input tax credit. Your bookkeeping needs to reflect whether the tax was recoverable. If you are not registered, the tax paid may form part of your business expense instead.

HST rules can get more detailed when you have mixed-use purchases, exempt sales, or multiple provinces involved. Clean invoices and organized records are the starting point either way.

## Records that make deductions easier to defend

Receipts matter, but they are not the whole story. You also need records that show the business purpose and the amount claimed. Save invoices, contracts, bank statements, mileage logs, and proof of payment. Keep digital copies in folders by month or expense category so you are not sorting through hundreds of screenshots in April.

A separate business bank account and credit card can be helpful, especially as your income grows. They are not a substitute for receipts, but they make it much easier to identify business transactions and avoid missing expenses.

For mixed-use costs, write down your method. If you claim 70% of your phone bill, be able to explain why. Consistency is more persuasive than choosing a different percentage each month based on what produces the largest deduction.

## Avoid the deductions that cause trouble

The most costly mistake is assuming a deduction is allowed because someone else claimed it. Tax results depend on facts. A home office can be valid for one consultant and unavailable to another. A vehicle claim can be substantial for a contractor visiting job sites and minimal for a writer who works entirely from home.

Be careful with cash purchases, personal shopping mixed with business items, and large round-number estimates. Do not claim costs you were reimbursed for, and do not deduct the personal part of a shared expense. If a purchase has an unusual business purpose, keep extra documentation.

Your self-employment income and expenses are generally reported on Form T2125. Filing accurately means matching the return to your records, not trying to force your records into a bigger refund.

If your books are behind, start with one month at a time. Gather bank and card statements, flag business transactions, collect receipts, and build a mileage total before preparing your return.
