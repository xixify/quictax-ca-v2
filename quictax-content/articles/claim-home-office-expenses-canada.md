# How to Claim Home Office Expenses in Canada

A spare bedroom, a kitchen table, or a corner of your living room can do more than support your workday. When you claim home office expenses in Canada, part of the cost of keeping that space can reduce your taxable income. The catch: the deduction has different rules for employees and self-employed people, and claiming the wrong costs can create a problem later.

The practical goal is simple. Claim the expenses you are entitled to, use a reasonable calculation, and keep documents that back it up. No guesswork. No inflated percentages. No missed deduction because the rules looked harder than they are.

## Who can claim home office expenses in Canada?

Your work status determines almost everything.

If you are self-employed, a freelancer, consultant, sole proprietor, or gig worker, you may deduct a reasonable portion of home expenses when you use part of your home to earn business income. Your workspace generally needs to be your principal place of business, or it must be used only to earn business income and used regularly to meet clients, customers, or patients.

For many self-employed Canadians, a dedicated office makes this straightforward. But a shared space can qualify too. If you work from your dining room during set business hours and use it personally the rest of the time, the expense is not automatically lost. You simply need to make a fair adjustment for both space and time.

Employees have a narrower path. To deduct workspace-in-the-home expenses, you generally must work from home more than 50% of the time for at least four consecutive weeks in the year, or use the workspace only to earn employment income and regularly meet clients or customers there. Your employer also needs to complete and sign Form T2200, Declaration of Conditions of Employment.

The temporary flat-rate method that helped many employees through the pandemic is no longer available for recent tax years. Most employees now use the detailed method, which means calculating actual eligible costs and retaining receipts. If your employer will not provide a T2200, do not assume you can claim the deduction.

## Start with the right workspace percentage

The [Canada Revenue Agency](https://quictax.ca/cra) expects a reasonable allocation, not a perfect one. The most common calculation starts with the area of your workspace compared with the total finished area of your home.

If your home is 1,000 square feet and your dedicated office is 100 square feet, your space percentage is 10%. If the space is used exclusively for work, you may generally apply that 10% to eligible household expenses.

Shared spaces need a second step. Say you use a 100-square-foot dining area in a 1,000-square-foot home for eight hours a day, five days a week. The space portion is 10%, but it is not used for work all the time. You would also adjust for the hours used for business. This produces a smaller, more realistic deduction - and a calculation that is easier to defend.

Keep a simple note showing your measurements, work schedule, and formula. You do not need a complicated spreadsheet, but you should be able to explain where your percentage came from if asked.

## Which home office costs can you deduct?

Eligible expenses depend on whether you are an employee or self-employed. The category matters as much as the receipt.

### Self-employed expenses

Self-employed taxpayers can generally claim the business-use portion of costs such as rent, electricity, heat, water, home internet access, home insurance, property taxes, maintenance, and repairs. If you own your home, you may also be able to allocate mortgage interest. The mortgage principal itself is not deductible.

Repairs must relate to the workspace or benefit the whole home. Repainting your office may be fully deductible as a business expense if the room is used only for business. Repairing the furnace benefits the entire home, so you would normally claim only your workspace percentage. A renovation that improves your home rather than maintains it is usually treated differently and may be a capital expense.

Internet deserves special attention. You can claim the business portion of your monthly internet service, but not the cost of buying a premium plan that is clearly beyond your business needs. Use a reasonable percentage based on actual work use, especially if the connection is shared with family members.

### Employee expenses

Employees using the detailed method may generally claim a reasonable work-related portion of rent, utilities, home internet access fees, minor maintenance, and certain supplies. Commission employees may have access to additional deductions, including a portion of home insurance and property taxes in some cases.

Most salaried employees cannot deduct mortgage interest, property taxes, or home insurance simply because they work from home. They also cannot claim the full cost of furniture, a desk, or major home upgrades as workspace expenses. The signed T2200 sets out the conditions of your employment, but it does not make every home-related cost deductible.

## Do not confuse current expenses with capital costs

This is where otherwise solid claims can go sideways. A current expense keeps your workspace running: utilities, cleaning supplies, a small repair, or monthly rent. A capital cost provides a longer-term benefit, such as a major renovation, a new addition, or certain equipment.

Self-employed people may be able to claim capital cost allowance, often called CCA, on eligible business assets. But claiming CCA on your home can have consequences. It may affect the principal residence exemption when you eventually sell, especially if the claim is substantial or changes the use of your property. For many home-based business owners, claiming a fair share of operating expenses is useful while avoiding CCA on the home itself.

A laptop, monitor, phone, or office chair may also have separate tax treatment from workspace expenses. Keep those purchases categorized separately so they are not accidentally included twice.

## Records matter more than a big deduction

A home office claim should tell a believable story: you worked there, you paid the expense, and your percentage is reasonable. Save rent receipts, utility bills, internet invoices, insurance statements, mortgage interest records, repair invoices, and your workspace calculation.

Digital copies are fine if they are clear and complete. Create one folder for each tax year and label documents as they arrive. For self-employed people, it also helps to separate business purchases from personal spending throughout the year. That makes tax filing faster and avoids the familiar March scramble through bank statements.

If you are an employee, retain your signed T2200 along with receipts. If you are self-employed, keep records supporting both the business expense and the income you earned from the business. The CRA can ask to see supporting documents after you file, so do not rely on a number you remember entering months ago.

## Common mistakes that cost Canadians money

The first mistake is claiming 100% of a household bill when the whole household benefits from it. The second is skipping the deduction entirely because the office is not a separate room. Shared spaces can qualify, but they require an honest time-based calculation.

Another common issue is treating personal expenses as business expenses. A family internet plan, a kitchen renovation, or a mortgage payment is not automatically deductible because you answer emails at home. Only the eligible business portion belongs on your return.

Finally, do not use an estimated percentage without documenting it. A modest claim supported by measurements and receipts is far stronger than a larger claim with no calculation behind it.

## Make the claim fit your real work setup

A home office deduction is not about finding the biggest possible number. It is about accurately reflecting what your work cost you. If you freelance from a dedicated room, your calculation may be clean and substantial. If you work for an employer from a shared table two days a week, the claim may be smaller or unavailable. Both outcomes are normal.
